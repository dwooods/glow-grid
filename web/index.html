Barely lit means the pixel isn't black but ends up at 1–3 out of 255 on the LED, so it reads as off or a faint smudge. Use pure black for "off", or brighten it.

Tools

Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y. Erase sets a pixel to black, which is "off" on the panel.

LED settings

Adjust these until the LED sim looks right. Saved PNGs remember them, and play.py on the Pi uses them for that image, so the panel matches what you see here. The Pi caps brightness at MAX_BRIGHTNESS (0.4 by default, about 6 A for full white). The sim uses the same rounding and "keep non-black pixels lit" rule. It's scaled so full white looks full on screen, so judge shapes and colors, not how bright it is.

How images turn out on the panel

The Pi fits any image to the grid and plays it. How good it looks depends on what you give it.

Works well

  • Pixel art at or near grid size (drawn here, in Piskel, or the examples) comes out pixel-perfect.
  • Photos and large images: anything more than 2× the grid is averaged per cell, so photos come out smooth instead of noisy.
  • Animations: an animated GIF, or a sprite sheet (frames side by side, as tall as the grid) named with the speed, walk_8fps.png, or _sheet.
  • Transparency becomes "off" LEDs.
  • Non-square images are centered and fitted without stretching.

Watch out for

  1. Mid-size pixel art (between 1× and 2× the grid, e.g. a 24×24 sprite) keeps exact pixels, so some detail is dropped. Draw at grid size, or at an exact 2×, 3×… upscale.
  2. Unnamed sprite sheets play as one squashed still. Add _8fps or _sheet to the name. Sheets saved from here already have it.
  3. Wiring: until the probe settings are set and confirmed on the Pi (glow-grid probe, glow-grid probe check, then k in the menu), images may look scrambled; the menu warns you.
  4. Near-black and dark colors: near-black isn't off, and dark shades can vanish. The Panel check outlines both.

Export to the Pi

Use 1× for the Pi: one pixel per LED. Larger scales make sharp, blocky copies for viewing and sharing, named _x16 etc.; don't copy a scaled animation to the Pi, it would play as one squashed still. Saved PNGs include this brightness and gamma, both inside the file and in its name (_bright35_gamma2.2). Copy to the Pi from PowerShell, then pick it from the glow-grid menu:
